# Seville marmalade

Makes about six jars. Takes two days, most of which is waiting.

![Finished jars on a windowsill](diagram.png)

## Ingredients

- 1 kg Seville oranges
- 2 kg granulated sugar
- 2 lemons
  - juice only
  - keep the pips, they matter
- 2.5 litres water

## Method

1. **Day one.** Halve the oranges and juice them. Keep everything — the juice,
   the pips, the pulp, the shells.

2. Tie the pips and pulp into a square of muslin. This is where the pectin
   comes from, and without it you have orange syrup.

3. Shred the shells as finely as you can stand. *Finely* is the whole
   difference between marmalade and chunky orange jam.

4. Put the juice, the shreds, the muslin bag and the water into the largest
   pan you own. Leave it overnight.

5. **Day two.** Simmer for about two hours, until a shred crushes between your
   fingers with no resistance at all.

6. Lift out the muslin bag. When it is cool enough to hold, squeeze it hard
   back into the pan — that is the set.

7. Add the sugar off the heat and stir until it has completely dissolved.
   Then, and only then, bring it back to a hard boil.

8. Test for a set after twelve minutes:

   > Cold saucer, one teaspoon, one minute in the freezer. Push it with a
   > finger. If it wrinkles, it is done. If it floods back, give it three more
   > minutes and try again.

9. Jar it hot, lid it hot, label it when cool.

## Notes

Seville oranges are in the shops for about three weeks in January. Buy twice
what you need and freeze the rest whole — they behave exactly the same.
